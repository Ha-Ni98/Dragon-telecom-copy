
# THE SETS

**Order:** S34 → S35 → S36. They are independent; this order puts the broken filters first because they block day-to-day use.
**Standing context:** the app and database are **not in production** — test data only. Schemas may change and migrations be created freely, but **no data backfill or repair script is wanted**; corrected logic applies going forward.

## S34 — Sales filters & POS-client parity

### Sales page — the filters don't work

Only the product search works. Every other filter on the page appears to do nothing.

**Diagnose before fixing.** For each filter the page offers — date range, seller, POS client, status, and any client-name search — determine whether it is **broken** or **absent**, and if broken, where the chain breaks:

- the filter's state never reaches the query key, so the query never refetches;
- the parameters are not serialised into the request;
- the backend query schema strips them (an unknown key is silently dropped in this codebase, so a renamed or mistyped parameter fails quietly);
- the service receives them and never applies them to the `where` clause.

The product search works, so compare the working path against each broken one — the difference is the bug.

**Client-name search.** The page's client filter may be a picker rather than a text search, in which case searching *by name* was never implemented. If so, add it: a text search matching the POS client's **name or phone**, consistent with the search used elsewhere for POS clients.

**Check for the same bug class elsewhere.** The admin list pages share a filter-bar pattern. Once the cause is known, audit the orders, invoices, repairs and purchasing list pages for the identical defect and fix every instance — a filter that silently does nothing is indistinguishable from a page with no data.

### POS clients — search and payment parity

- **Add a search by client name** to the POS clients list on **both** the admin and staff pages. A search endpoint already exists for attaching a client at the POS (by name or phone); check whether the *list* endpoint supports a search parameter and add it if not, rather than reusing the attach-flow endpoint for a paginated table.
- **Add the record-payment action to the staff POS clients page**, matching the admin one: amount above zero, and a dialog showing the client's **previous credit and resulting new credit** before and after submitting.
- **Widen the guard.** Recording a client payment is currently gated on `STAFF_INVOICES`. Holding **`STAFF_POS_CLIENTS` must now be sufficient** for this action, so a staff member who manages POS clients can take a payment without also holding the invoices page. Update the route guard, and check whether any other staff surface depends on the old gate before changing it.
- The payment itself follows the existing rules unchanged: a `ClientPayment` row written in-transaction on a locked row, with previous/new credit snapshots, `totalPaid` moving only through the ledger.

## S35 — Cash register: profit, standalone payments & the staff dashboard

### Profit

Add to the cash register:

- **Sales profit** — Σ `totalProfit` over the day's completed sales.
- **Repair profit** — Σ `totalProfit` over the day's picked-up repairs.
- **Net profit** = sales profit + repair profit **− profit reversed by returns** (sales returns and repair returns of that day).

**Supplier payments are not an expense and must not reduce net profit.** Buying stock is a balance-sheet movement; its cost reaches the P&L later, as FIFO cost of goods, when the item is sold. Subtracting supplier payments here would make a large delivery day read as a catastrophic loss. State this in the plan so it isn't "corrected" later.

### Standalone payments

Add a **Payments** line covering money that moves without a sale or a repair behind it:

- **Client payments with no linked invoice** (`invoiceId` null) — debt settlement against a client's credit — as **(+)**.
- **Supplier payments with no linked invoice** (`supplierInvoiceId` null) — on-account payments to a supplier — as **(−)**.

**Every payment row must be counted exactly once across the whole page.** The rule is: a payment **linked to an invoice** belongs to its document's section (Sales, Repairs or Suppliers); an **unlinked** payment belongs to the new Payments line. Before writing the plan, read what the Suppliers section's "total paid to suppliers" currently counts — if it counts *all* supplier payments of the day, it will double-count against the new line, and must be narrowed to invoice-linked ones. Say explicitly in the plan which query owns which rows.

With that rule the page sums to real cash movement, and the plan should present it that way:

```
+ paid on the day's sales        + paid on the day's repair pickups
+ standalone client payments     − refunds paid out
− supplier payments (invoice-linked and standalone)
= net cash movement for the day
```

### Staff dashboard

Put **the whole cash register on the staff dashboard**, for the same day, so a staff member can see what should be in the drawer.

- **Whole-store figures, not the staff member's own.** The cash register reports a physical drawer, so scoping it per seller would report a number that matches nothing. This is a deliberate exception to the rule that staff see only their own documents — state it in the plan.
- **Every profit figure is excluded** from the staff view — sales profit, repair profit and net profit alike — consistent with margins being admin-only.
- **Strip the profit fields in the mapper**, not in the component. A staff-facing DTO that carries profit and merely doesn't render it is a leak, and the established rule in this codebase is that the mapper is where margins are removed.
- Reuse the cash register's existing aggregation rather than writing a second one; the staff variant differs only in its mapper and its guard.

Day boundaries stay **Africa/Algiers local time**, and each section stays anchored to the timestamp already established for it (sales by `soldAt`, repairs by `pickedUpAt`, orders by completion, supplier invoices by confirmation date, payments by their own `paidAt`).

## S36 — Client purchase history: sold-products tab

A new tab on the **client information page**, for **admin and staff** — not on the client's own account page.

The staff equivalent may be a client-information page or the POS-client detail view; determine which exists from the code and say in the plan where the tab lands on each side.

### The table

Every product the client has ever bought, aggregated across:

- **POS sales** — completed sales attached to the client's POS identity;
- **Online orders** — completed orders attached to their web identity.

Draft sales and orders that have not reached completion are excluded, consistent with when the app recognises a sale. **Repair parts are excluded** — those are components consumed in a service, not products the client bought.

Columns: product name, **quantity bought**, **total amount**, and — shown **separately, not netted** — **quantity returned** and **amount refunded**. A client who bought three and returned one reads as "3 bought, 1 returned", never as "2".

Server-side pagination, following the pattern the other admin tables already use.

### The drill-down

Clicking a row opens the documents behind that product: each sale or order that contained it, with its **date, reference, quantity, unit price and line total** — so "display iPhone 13 — 3 units" expands to "2 on the sale of 12/11/2025, 1 on the sale of 20/12/2025". Any **returns** of that product are listed in the same view as their own entries, with their date, reference and refunded amount.

Each row links through to the full sale or order.

### Backend

- A new endpoint on the clients module returning the paginated aggregate, plus a second for one product's documents (or one endpoint with a product parameter — the plan decides and says why).
- The aggregation runs **in SQL** — a `GROUP BY` over sale lines and order lines with the pagination applied in the query. Do not load every line a client has ever bought into memory to group it in JavaScript; the machine has 1GB and one shared CPU.
- Unioning two sources means care with the join: sales reach the client through `posClientId`, orders through the web `clientId`. A walk-in POS client has only sales; a web client has both. Handle the client with no linked POS identity, and the POS client with no web account, without a crash or a silently empty table.
- Amounts come from the **stored line figures** (`unitPrice`, `lineTotal`), never recomputed from current catalog prices — a product repriced since the sale must still show what the client actually paid.
- **Cost and profit never appear in this tab**, on either side.

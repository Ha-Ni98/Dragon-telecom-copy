# THE SETS

**Order:** S30 → S31 → S32 → S33.

S30 fixes the repair reservation defects that S31's picker would otherwise inherit; S32's return logic is independent of both; S33 reads everything the first three produce, so it goes last.

---

## S30 — Repair lifecycle: reservations, cancellation, return & receipts

### The reservation defects (fix these first — the rest builds on them)

- **Deleting a part from a repair leaves its reservation behind.** The `PENDING_REPAIR` movement stays in the ledger and `quantityReserved` is never decremented, so the stock is held by a part nobody is using. Deleting a part must release its reservation in the same transaction. Audit the add/delete/edit-quantity paths together — changing a part's quantity has the same exposure.
- Verify the reservation is released on **every** exit path: part deleted, repair cancelled, repair returned, and repair picked up (where release is immediately followed by consumption).

### Cancellation

A repair may be cancelled from `INTAKE`, `PROCESSING` or `COMPLETED` — **never from `PICKED_UP`**, whose invoice is immutable; reversing that is a return, not a cancel.

- **New movement type `REPAIR_CANCELLED`, direction `PENDING`.** One row per part, decrementing **`quantityReserved`** and leaving **`quantityOnHand` untouched**.
  **This is critical: a repair cancelled before pickup consumed nothing — the parts never left the shelf.** Incrementing on-hand here would inflate stock by the entire parts list of every cancelled repair and create phantom FIFO layers with no purchase behind them. The row exists for ledger traceability ("released because REP-xxxx was cancelled"), distinguishable from a plain `PENDING_RELEASE`; the arithmetic is release-only.
- **No invoice exists at cancellation** (invoices are issued at pickup), so there is nothing to cancel on the document side.
- **Payments taken before cancellation are reversed, never deleted.** Each is reversed by a **negative-amount `ClientPayment` row** linked to the original, per the ledger rules. The repair's `paidAmount` drops accordingly, and the payments page shows both what was taken and what was returned. The existing cancel-time refund prompt keeps its default of the amount paid.
- **Labour price:** `Technician` carries no payout, commission or cost model, so labour is store revenue rather than a technician expense. Nothing is owed back; the requirement is simply that a cancelled repair stops counting toward revenue. Confirm statistics excludes it.

### Return — a new terminal state

- **Reachable only from `PICKED_UP`.** No other transition leads to it.
- **Per-part restock choice**, like sales returns: the dialog lists the consumed parts with a restock toggle each, since a screen that was fitted and removed may not be resellable.
- **New movement type `REPAIR_RETURN`, direction `IN`**, one row per **restocked** part, incrementing `quantityOnHand` **at the cost it was consumed at** (read from the consumption rows, not from the current average). Parts marked not-restocked produce no movement. Here the quantity increase is correct, because pickup did consume them — this is the difference from cancellation.
- **The invoice is cancelled** (status only; the number is never reused and gaps are never closed) and **every payment against it is reversed by a negative-amount ledger row**. The POS client's `credit`, `totalPaid` and `totalSold` adjust accordingly, all in-transaction on locked rows.
- Returned repairs stop counting toward revenue, cost and profit in statistics.
- A returned repair is **read-only**, like a picked-up or cancelled one.

### Receipts and tickets

- **The pickup receipt shows the total only** — not the parts breakdown, not the labour line. One figure.
- **The claim ticket shows the total at whatever stage it is printed**, since the technician may print at any step: when nothing is priced yet the total is 0 and **the price is omitted entirely** rather than printed as zero; once parts or labour are priced, the current total appears.

### Ripple

Both new movement types need the contract enum, the movement-ledger filter options, their label translations, and a line in the ledger's type legend. The return state needs the status enum, the transition map, the board column or filter, and its own translations.

## S31 — Shared product-picker dialog *(repairs & supplier invoices)*

One dialog component, two consumers. Build it once, parameterised.

### Shape

- Opened by an **Add products** button that replaces the current inline search field.
- A **search input** at the top, using the same server-side search as the field it replaces (name or barcode, debounced, whole catalog — not a recent-20 slice).
- Beneath it a **table** of matching products: name, quantity, and prices (per consumer, below), with a **checkbox per row**.
- **Pagination at 10 rows per page** with explicit **previous/next** buttons.
- **When the search box is empty the table shows products** (not an empty state) so the user can browse straight away.
- **Scrollable body** with the search and the action bar staying in view.
- A way to **clear all selections** at once, and a visible count of what is currently selected.
- **Add** and **Cancel** at the bottom. Cancel discards the selection; **Add closes the dialog and hands the selected products to the existing list below the field** — the one where quantity and price stay editable until the user presses the existing save/add-parts action. The dialog replaces only the *finding* of products, not the confirming of them.
- Selections survive paging and re-searching within one dialog session.

### Repairs consumer

- Price columns: **all four prices**, as on the POS product card (default plus the three category prices).
- **Products with quantity 0 are disabled** and cannot be checked — you cannot fit a part you don't have.

### Supplier-invoice consumer

- Price columns: **`avgPrice` and `avgStockPrice`** instead of sale prices — the buyer is recalling what the product previously cost, not what it sells for.
- **Quantity 0 is selectable.** Receiving stock for an out-of-stock product is the normal case, so the repairs rule must not leak here.
- Keeps the **quick-add-new-product** button established earlier, opening the existing create dialog and returning the new product selected.

This replaces the product selection on the add-supplier-invoice page; everything else on that page stays as it is.

## S32 — POS & sales: payment validation, search and returns

### POS payment validation

- The **paid amount starts empty** — never prefilled with the subtotal — so the cashier must enter what the customer actually handed over.
- **Walk-in sale (no client attached):** paid must **equal the total exactly**. Empty, less, or more blocks the sale with an explanatory message. With no client there is nowhere to post a credit or an overpayment, so the till must balance.
- **Attached client:** empty blocks the sale. Any amount **different from the total — below or above — opens a confirmation dialog** showing the previous credit and the resulting new credit, and the sale proceeds only on confirmation. Underpayment raises his credit; overpayment reduces it (and may take it negative, meaning the store owes him).
- Verify the resulting **`ClientPayment` row** is written correctly in the confirm transaction: right amount, linked to the issued invoice, with previous/new credit snapshots, and `totalPaid` moving only through that row.

### Sales page

- **Search by sold product** — returns every sale containing that product, by name or barcode.
- A **Today's sales** button applying a single-click date filter for the current day in **Africa/Algiers local time**.

### Returns

- The **refund amount starts empty**, mirroring the POS rule: walk-in returns must refund **exactly** the line's sold price; a return for an attached client must not be empty, and any amount differing from the sold price opens the same **confirmation dialog** showing previous and new credit.
- **Return the whole sale** — a new action on the sale view that returns every line at once, with one refund amount field following the same validation.
- **Cache arithmetic**, applied per returned line, in-transaction on locked rows:
  - `credit += (refundAmount − linePrice)`
  - `totalPaid −= refundAmount` — effected by a **negative-amount `ClientPayment` row**, never a direct decrement, since the ledger owns `totalPaid`
  - `totalSold −= linePrice`
  **Do not derive `totalSold` from `credit + totalPaid`.** That identity only holds for a POS client with no opening `initialCredit`, and the drift would be silent. Compute it directly.
  The credit formula generalises correctly: a client who never paid has his debt erased with no cash out; a client who paid in full gets cash back with credit unchanged; a refund-to-credit leaves the store owing him.
- Confirm the **restock** behavior still follows the existing rule — restocked lines re-enter at the consumed cost, non-restocked lines produce no movement.

## S33 — Cash register (Caisse) page *(admin only; needs S30, S32)*

A new sidebar entry, **admin only**, reporting the day's activity.

- Defaults to **today**, with a date picker for any other day.
- **Day boundaries are Africa/Algiers local time**, not UTC — a sale at 00:30 local belongs to that day. The plan must state how the boundary is computed and make sure every section uses the same one.
- Each section is anchored to the timestamp that marks the money moving: **sales by `soldAt`**, **repairs by `pickedUpAt`**, **orders by their completion timestamp**, **supplier invoices by their confirmation date**.

**Sections:**

| Section | Figures |
|---|---|
| **Sales** | number of sales · total paid · total new credit (Σ sale totals − Σ paid) · total sold |
| **Returns** | number of returns · total refunded |
| **Repairs** | number of repairs · unpaid repair amount · paid repair amount · refunded repair amount (where any) |
| **Orders** | confirmed and in-delivery orders of the day · completed orders of the day · total paid on completed orders |
| **Suppliers** | number of invoices (count) · total paid to suppliers · total new supplier credit · total invoiced (sum) |

- Every figure is read from **stored** values — sale totals, ledger payment rows, repair totals, order totals — never recomputed from current averages or live prices.
- Repair and return figures must reflect **S30's** reversal rules: a cancelled or returned repair's payments appear as negative ledger rows, so refund totals follow naturally and paid totals net out.
- Each section's headline figure should **drill through** to the underlying documents filtered to that day, so the owner can see what made up a number.
- Follow the statistics module's existing query and card patterns rather than inventing a new aggregation path, and keep the aggregation **in SQL** — the machine has one shared CPU.
